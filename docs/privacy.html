Privacy Policy

OpenHead Robot Face for iOS and iPadOS · Effective 1 November 2026 (location and barometer added)

In short: the app collects no personal data. It has no accounts, analytics, advertising or tracking, and it sends nothing to us or to any third party. It only talks to devices on your own local network that you have paired with it.

Who we are

OpenHead is an open-source project published on GitHub at github.com/PatrikBichis/openhead. "We" in this policy means the developer of the OpenHead Robot Face app. Questions about this policy can be asked on the support page.

Data we collect

None. The app does not collect, store on our behalf, sell or share personal data, and it contains no third-party SDKs for analytics, crash reporting or advertising.

What the app does on your device and network

  • Local network. The app runs a small server on your Wi-Fi so that a robot controller can connect, and announces itself with Bonjour (mDNS) so it can be found. iOS asks for permission to use the local network. Connections come only from devices on the same network.
  • Pairing. A controller must type a 6-digit code shown on the screen. Each paired controller gets a random token, stored with its name and last-seen time in the device's Keychain. Nothing about it leaves the device. You can revoke any controller in the app's settings.
  • Motion sensors. If a paired controller subscribes, the app streams the device's orientation, rotation and acceleration to that controller, on your local network only. This needs no permission and is not stored.
  • Pictures, video and web pages. When a paired controller asks the app to show an image, a video or a web page, the app fetches it from the address the controller gives. That server sees the request as it would from any browser. The app does not keep the content after it is no longer shown.
  • Logs and screenshots. The app keeps a short log of its own activity in memory (cleared when the app quits) for troubleshooting. With dev mode switched on in settings, a paired controller can read that log and take screenshots of what is on screen. Dev mode is off by default.
  • Settings. Your settings (name, port, theme, mounting, dev mode) are stored on the device only.
  • Camera. The camera is only used when a paired controller switches on a camera mode, and iOS asks for your permission the first time. In capture mode the front camera shows what the head sees on its own screen and finds faces; in sensing mode ARKit uses the cameras to track where the head is in the room and to follow one face. Camera images are processed on the device and are never stored or sent anywhere. What paired controllers receive are positions and angles: where faces are (direction, size, distance) and where the head is. Faces are not recognised or identified, and no face data is kept. With dev mode switched on, a paired controller can take a screenshot of the screen, which then includes the camera view.
  • Location. Only when a paired controller subscribes to the gps stream does the app ask iOS for your location ("While Using the App"), and iOS asks for your permission the first time. The location is sent only to the paired controllers on your local network that asked for it, while they are connected. It is not stored, not sent to us and not used for anything else. With location allowed, the compass also reports true north.
  • Compass, magnetometer and barometer. The heading on the camera view comes from the compass. If a paired controller subscribes to the env stream, the app sends it the compass heading, the magnetic field and the air pressure and altitude change from the barometer, on your local network only. The barometer needs Motion & Fitness permission, which iOS asks for the first time. None of this is stored.
  • Microphone. The app does not use the microphone.

Children

The app collects no data from anyone, including children.

Data retention and deletion

Everything the app stores stays on your device. Deleting the app removes its settings and pairing tokens; revoking a controller in settings removes its token immediately.

Security

Only paired controllers can change what the app shows; five wrong pairing codes lock pairing for five minutes. Traffic on the local network is not encrypted in this version, so use the app on a network you trust.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version is published on this page with a new effective date, and the history is kept in the project's public Git repository.
