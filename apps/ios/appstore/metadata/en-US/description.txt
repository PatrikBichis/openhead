OpenHead turns an iPhone or iPad into the face of a robot.

Mount the phone in landscape on your robot, connect it to the same Wi-Fi as the robot's computer, and the robot can make the face smile, frown, look around, speak with its own voice, listen and show things. Anything that can open a WebSocket can drive it: a Python script, a robot's onboard computer, or an AI agent.

The phone also gives the robot its cameras and sensors. Instead of wiring up a camera, an IMU and a tracking system, a robot builder gets face tracking, motion data, GPS, a barometer, a compass and the head's position in the room from one device they already own, over the same connection.

FACE
• 28 expressions shared by every theme: happy, curious, thinking, surprised, sad, angry, sleepy, scared, confused, shocked, excited, bored, skeptical, worried, proud, laughing, nervous, smug and more.
• Blend expressions, hold them for a moment, or layer a short reaction on top of a mood.
• Three themes: Cartoon, Minimal (light bars, low power) and Halloween.
• The eyes follow a point on the screen or a direction from the head; blinking, breathing and small eye movements keep the face alive when nothing happens.

VOICE, LISTENING AND SUBTITLES
• The head speaks with its own voice (Apple's voices, on the phone); the mouth follows the sound and the words show as subtitles.
• Cues in the text act at the right moment: a smile, a laugh after a joke, a serious face for news.
• Speech to text with the microphone, on the device where the language allows; the face looks attentive while you talk. The text goes to the robot or AI agent.
• Or the robot streams its own voice level and the mouth follows. Subtitles show an AI answer word by word.

PICTURES, VIDEO AND WEB PAGES
• Show an image (PNG, JPEG, GIF including animated, WebP, SVG), a video or a web page full screen, or keep the face running in a corner while it shows.
• Files on the robot's computer are sent straight to the head over your network.

CAMERAS AND HUD
• Show the front camera with a sci-fi heads-up display over it: boxes around faces, a locked target, compass, artificial horizon, battery and temperature. Four HUD themes, and the robot can add its own values, text and instruments.
• Faces: the robot gets each person's bearing, elevation and size 15 times a second, and the eyes can follow them.
• Screenshots of what the head shows, camera view included.

POSITION AND SENSORS
• World pose from ARKit (visual-inertial SLAM): the head's position in metres and its orientation in the room, up to 60 times a second, with tracking state. Distance to a face in sensing mode.
• Motion sensor stream (orientation, rotation, acceleration) up to 100 times a second, in the robot's conventions.
• GPS: latitude, longitude, altitude, accuracy, speed and course once a second, after you allow location access.
• Barometer (air pressure, altitude change), magnetometer and compass heading up to 10 times a second.
• Battery, temperature state and connected clients.
• Camera images and audio are never stored. Location, sensor readings, recognised speech and a small camera stream (only when a paired viewer asks; the face shows "Camera shared") go only to the robot controllers you paired, on your network.

SAFE ON YOUR NETWORK
• A controller must be paired with a 6-digit code shown on the screen; each one gets its own token, which you can revoke in settings.
• Everything stays on your local network. No accounts, no cloud, no tracking.

FOR DEVELOPERS
• Open protocol (JSON over WebSocket) with a published schema, a Python library and command line tool, a desktop simulator, and an MCP server so AI agents can drive the head and check their work with screenshots and logs.
• Open source under the Apache 2.0 license: github.com/PatrikBichis/openhead

The app shows the face on its own; to change it, run the OpenHead tools (or your own code) on a computer on the same network. Long-press the face for 1.5 seconds to open settings.
